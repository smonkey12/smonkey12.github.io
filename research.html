Research & Publications

I study how training, parameterization, data, and feedback shape what AI systems learn and how effectively they perform. A central theme is that seemingly small design choices can produce large statistical consequences.

I have shown how attention parameterization changes implicit bias and generalization, how attention heads specialize during training, when learned features can be genuinely recovered, and when synthetic data and verification improve, or fail to improve, a pretrained model. My recent work extends this perspective to agentic systems, where feedback and external computation can substantially change reasoning performance.

My goal is to turn these mechanisms into principles for designing more reliable, interpretable, and efficient AI systems.
